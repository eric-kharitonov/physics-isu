03 / DEFINITION

A spacecraft uses the gravity of a moving massive body to change its path — with almost no fuel.

Also called a slingshot, swing-by, or flyby. The "assist" is just the body's gravity pulling on the craft as it passes.

06 / VOCABULARY

Six words to keep — we'll point back to each one.

Gravitational field
The region around a mass where it pulls on other masses.
Sphere of influence
The bubble where the Moon's gravity wins over Earth's. Orion crossed into it on the way in.
Flyby / swing-by
Passing close to a body without orbiting or landing on it.
Free-return trajectory
A path shaped so gravity loops you home on its own — a built-in safety net if the engine fails.
Kinetic energy  Ek
Energy of motion. Ek = ½mv². Faster = more.
Gravitational PE  Eg
Stored energy from position. Closer to the mass = lower.

07 / THE MISSION

Ten days, four astronauts, one engine burn that matters.

APR 1 · LAUNCH
SLS lifts Orion + 4 crew into Earth orbit. First crewed lunar flight in 50+ years.
TLI BURN
Engine fires to leave Earth orbit. Sets the figure-eight free-return path toward the Moon.
COAST OUT
Engines off — gravity does the work. Orion crosses into the Moon's sphere of influence.
APR 6 · FLYBY
Swings around the far side, ≈ 6,545 km up. The Moon's gravity bends the path back toward Earth.
APR 10 · HOME
Coasts back and re-enters — splashdown. No big "come-home" burn needed.

09 / CONNECTION TO SPH3U

Nothing new — just four things you already learned this year.

Newton's 1st law — inertia
Orion would fly straight forever if no force acted. Gravity is what curves it.
Newton's 2nd law — F = ma
Gravity is an unbalanced force → it causes acceleration. Here, mostly a change of direction.
Conservation of energy
Kinetic and potential energy trade back and forth; their sum stays constant.
Kinematics
A curved 2-D path under a force — the same logic as projectile motion, wrapped around the Moon.

12 / EQUATION 3 — THE SPEED
Ek + Eg = constant
W = ΔEk = ½mv22 − ½mv12

14 / PROBLEM 1 — THE FORCE  ·  GRASP

At closest approach, how hard does the Moon pull Orion — and what acceleration is that?

Given
m₁ = MMoon = 7.35×10²² kg
m₂ = mOrion = 35,000 kg
r = 6,545 + 1,737 km = 8.28×10⁶ m
G = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²
Required  ·  Analysis
Force F, then acceleration a.

F = G·m₁·m₂ / r²
a = F / m₂
Solution
F = (6.67×10⁻¹¹)(7.35×10²²)(35,000) / (8.28×10⁶)² = 1.72×10¹⁷ / 6.86×10¹³
F ≈ 2.5 × 10³ N
a ≈ 0.072 m/s²
Elvin
SMALL PULL · ACTS OVER THOUSANDS OF KM
15 / PROBLEM 2 — THE SPEED  ·  GRASP

Enter at v₁ = 1000 m/s. If gravity does W = +1.5×10¹⁰ J pulling inward, how fast at closest approach?

Given  ·  Required
m = 35,000 kg   v₁ = 1000 m/s
W = +1.5×10¹⁰ J (illustrative)

Find v₂ at closest approach.
Analysis  ·  Solution
v₂ = √( v₁² + 2W/m )
½mv₁² = 1.75×10¹⁰ J
½mv₂² = 1.75 + 1.50 = 3.25×10¹⁰ J
v₂ ≈ 1360 m/s

Climbing out, gravity does an equal −1.5×10¹⁰ J → back to 1000 m/s. No permanent boost — just redirected home.

16 / THE 30-SECOND VERSION

The Moon's gravity (F = Gm₁m₂/r²) acted as a steering force (F = ma). Orion sped up falling in, slowed back down climbing out, and left at the same speed it arrived.

So gravity changed its direction — not its speed.
